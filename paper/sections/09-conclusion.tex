\section{Conclusion}

\project{} treats semantic equivalence as the first-class obstacle to proving
a legacy game's execution.  The initial replay runner demonstrates that the
reconstructed engine can consume real v1.02h witnesses deterministically and
complete full runs, while strict parsing removes several ambient-memory and
format-trust hazards.  A first shipped-executable anchor now agrees for 2,000
frames on a 50-field semantic projection.  The result is not yet a proof system and
does not yet establish whole-state equivalence with the shipped executable.

A first \code{no\_std} player-position microkernel turns that anchor into an
executable proof target.  It avoids native floating point, implements explicit
integer PC24 rounding, fails closed outside its stated domain, and matches all
1,999 consecutive transitions derived from the retail prefix.  This remains a
narrow translation-validation result, not a formal arithmetic or refinement
theorem and not a complete gameplay guest.  It is, however, now a real zkVM
target: an OpenVM v2.0.1 RV32IM adapter calls the same crate, commits its private
replay masks and complete endpoint with SHA-256, and produces a tracked
application proof bound to the exact executable.  An enclosing transition
derives the life-state/timer sequence, time-stop carry, speeds, rate, bounds,
and inactive-bomb multipliers instead of accepting eleven environment words
per frame.  A further transition derives focus, previous input, fire-timer
cadence, and 1,590 \code{SpawnBullets} requests while keeping the replay-only
payload.  Its 1,999-step statement costs 211,737,944 metered cells, proves in
75.57 seconds at roughly 46.8 GiB peak resident memory, and verifies in 0.11
seconds.  This follows two rejected or superseded boundaries: an uncommitted
existential transcript and a 95,976-byte hash-bound but prover-chosen
environment transcript.

A fourth guest now refines the local Reimu-A callback through power ranks
1--3, timer gates, the 80-slot allocator, and initialized geometry.  Across
1,590 retail-bound invocations it derives and publicly hashes 422 bullets,
costing 7,394,181 instructions and 309,751,316 cells; its tracked application
proof completes in 98.60 seconds and verifies in 0.12 seconds.  Crucially, each
pre-call slot array remains independently observed.  The result is therefore a
local function proof, not evidence that the guest already carries authentic
bullet liveness across frames.

A fifth guest closes that witness boundary for the maximal writer-local prefix.
Starting from the fixed empty frame-1 pool and 206 replay masks, it derives
Player motion and shooting cadence together with rank-1 bullet movement,
14-by-14 bounds reclamation, fixed ANM/timer behavior, and allocation through
frame 207.  Its 436-byte private payload contains no slot pre-states, and its
public digest commits all intermediate states, 35 initializations, and 30
reclamations.  The 206-step workload costs 10,859,285 instructions and
423,247,443 cells; proving takes 117.26 seconds at 51,101,780 KiB peak RSS and
verification takes 0.18 seconds.  The transition rejects another step because
EnemyManager first writes collided bullet state at frame 208.  This is a
soundness boundary, not an Enemy/ECL proof.

An experimental shared binary protocol now emits real per-frame subsystem and
root digests with raw floating-point bits, stable identities, and fail-closed
decoding.  Complete repeated Linux runs are byte-identical, and the first broad
ANM experiment already exposed the distinction between allocated memory and
future-live semantic state.  The retail probe already turns a mismatch in its
narrow root projection into a reproducible first-frame/first-field diagnosis.
The next concrete Player milestone is to compose Enemy/ECL state so the
frame-208 collision is derived, then add or rule out collision/death/respawn,
bomb, power, and item paths.  Deriving the fixed
post-calc anchor from registration/pre-stage scheduling, time-stop writers, and
non-full-speed paths remains necessary while resolving the
known reuse and dynamic-job omissions and enlarging the shipped-executable
canonical subsystem schema.

A first static arithmetic census now makes the floating-point gate concrete:
the mapped game surface contains thousands of x87 operations, 26 inline
\code{fsincos} sites, 1,793 stores, and numerous calls into conversion and
transcendental helpers.  The CRT's embedded \code{0x027f} control profile is
decoded in Lean together with the D3DX toward-zero profile, while retail
post-calc sampling observes ambient \code{0x007f} and makes a PC24 campaign the
next arithmetic requirement.  A pinned SoftFloat probe
matches 34,002,802 hardware result/condition/exception tuples across basic
arithmetic, stores, rounding, integer conversion, and both reached comparison
widths, after an initial denormal-priority rule was falsified and corrected.
All 244 mapped comparison consumers are classified into eleven checked truth
tables.  A temporary i386 harness additionally matches 1,000,014 in-domain
executions of the exact \code{\_\_ftol2} body against dyadic truncation, plus
1,000,154 focused executions against its ECL sentinel observation.  This
surface is indexed by a hash-bound ledger of 244 comparison and 77 helper
addresses.  A bit-mask re-audit records 75 full-EAX and two AL-only helper
observers, while a derived source ledger proposes 68 omission and nine retain
candidates.  For the ECL call, a pinned 25-way dispatch audit and Lean theorem
replace a blanket range goal with exact helper-to-classifier refinement.  This
same observation-driven analysis binds the eight point-item calls to four
score profiles and exposes why collision success cannot prove finiteness in
the presence of NaN.  A 37-instruction helper audit and Lean low-EAX theorem
instead support a total finite/infinity/NaN score model: exceptional invalid
conversion selects the bounded top branch, while finite collection gives the
geometric interval.  The model conditionally proves the resulting score
bounds; 2,081 Linux collections supply counterexample-search evidence for its
finite branch.  A dependent 95-instruction player audit and total Lean clamp
theorem further show that either infinity is repaired by the retail bounds and
reduce the former blanket player premise to candidate-not-NaN, writer,
scheduling, binary/x87, and code-binding obligations.  Inspecting ECL pointer
resolution then exposes an alias threat missed by direct-assignment search: two
handlers bypass the readonly guard.  A fixed-data walk finds none of 1,844
candidate outputs naming player position, including all 80 bypass operations,
while keeping extraction and runtime-decoder binding open.  This remains
conservative bookkeeping: startup state, load/remainder behavior,
noninterference, those player and ECL obligations, architectural helper
semantics, verified code correspondence, and
transcendental microarchitecture are not yet proved.  The
fidelity design therefore binds operations to original addresses and actual
observations instead of assuming source-level host arithmetic is exact.

That evidence will guide the projection, but it cannot prove a slice sound.
The intended end state additionally requires a machine-checked commuting
transition and noninterference argument, exact or refined arithmetic semantics,
and a reviewed binding from those definitions to both executables.  Only then
can broader zkVM performance results support the intended replay claim; the
current result supports only its explicitly hash-bound micro-slice.
